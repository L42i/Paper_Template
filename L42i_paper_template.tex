\section{Introduction}
Open with the idea. What is the project, and what motivated it? What
question, sound, gesture, or performance situation were you exploring?
Close this section with a one-sentence statement of what the paper
contributes or demonstrates, so the reader knows where things are
headed.

\section{Background and Related Work}
Situate your work. Mention the instruments, systems, pieces, composers,
or research that your project builds on or reacts to. This is where you
cite sources --- for example, the laptop-orchestra tradition
\cite{trueman2006plork}, foundational computer-music
technique~\cite{puckette2007}, or a tool you relied
on~\cite{supercollider}. Citations are numbered automatically; see the
\texttt{L42i\_paper\_template.bib} file for how each entry is written.

\section{System Design}
Describe the architecture of what you built at a conceptual level,
before the implementation details. What are the parts, and how does
signal or information flow between them? A block diagram is usually the
clearest way to show this (Figure~\ref{fig:signal-flow}).

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/signal-flow}
  \caption{A signal-flow diagram of the system: input is captured,
    mapped to synthesis parameters, rendered as sound, spatialized, and
    sent to the loudspeakers. Replace this with a diagram of your own
    system.}
  \Description{Five boxes connected left to right by arrows: Controller
    or Sensor, Mapping, Sound Synthesis, Spatialization, and Loudspeaker
    Array.}
  \label{fig:signal-flow}
\end{figure}

\section{Implementation}
Now get concrete. What software and hardware did you use (for example
Pure Data, SuperCollider, Max, sensors, microphones, a loudspeaker
array)? How did you map input to sound? If the mapping or a piece of
DSP is central to your project, show it. For instance, a simple
amplitude control that scales a gesture value $g \in [0,1]$ into a
gain~$a$ might be written as

\begin{equation}
  a = g^{\,2},
  \label{eq:mapping}
\end{equation}

which gives finer control at low levels. Refer back to equations by
number, as in Equation~\ref{eq:mapping}.

\section{Performance and Evaluation}
Describe how the work was actually used --- in rehearsal, in
performance, or in testing. What happened? What worked well, and what
did not? Concrete observations are more useful than general claims. A
small table can help summarize results or configurations
(Table~\ref{tab:setup}).

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Example table: summarize a setup, a mapping, or results.
    Replace the rows with your own.}
  \label{tab:setup}
  \begin{tabular}{ll}
    \toprule
    Element & Choice \\
    \midrule
    Controller   & Wireless motion sensor \\
    Synthesis    & Granular, real-time \\
    Spatializer  & 8-channel VBAP \\
    Latency      & $\approx$ 12\,ms \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\section{Discussion}
Step back and reflect. What did the project teach you about the idea
you started with? What were the limitations, and what would you change?
This is also the place to connect your specific result back to the
broader themes of the course.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
Briefly restate what you did and what it showed, then point to where it
could go next --- extensions, a new piece, a more robust version, a
different performance context.
